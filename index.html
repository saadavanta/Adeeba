About Adeeba Asif

A thoughtful, family-centred approach to nutrition.

My journey into nutrition began with a simple question: Why? From a young age, I often heard people recommend certain foods for fever, colds, or illness but rarely explain why they were recommended, when they should be eaten, or how much was appropriate. This curiosity led me to study Nutrition & Dietetics, not simply to learn what to eat, but to understand the science behind food, health, and everyday choices.

I believe nutrition should not be about strict rules, deprivation, guilt, or labeling foods as simply “good” or “bad.” Food is a part of everyday life, and healthy eating should be understood, enjoyed, and sustainable. Knowing what we eat, why we eat it, how much we need, when to eat it, and how it is prepared can make a meaningful difference to our health.

My work focuses on Pediatric Nutrition and Behavioral & Psychological Nutrition, with particular attention to helping parents understand the reasons behind their child’s eating behavior rather than simply labeling them as a “picky eater.” I believe every child deserves to be understood as an individual, with their appetite, preferences, environment, behavior, and relationship with food carefully considered.

Ultimately, I don’t want to simply tell people what to eat. I want to help them understand why. My goal is to provide practical, evidence-based guidance that helps individuals and families build healthier habits, a better relationship with food, and a clear path toward long-term wellbeing.

Common questions

Answers, without pressure.

My child only eats a few foods. Is this a phase?
Selective eating is common, and it can mean many different things. Rather than labelling it, the first step is understanding the pattern — what is happening at the table, how foods are offered, and what the child’s experience of mealtimes feels like. A calm, structured approach usually matters more than forcing variety.
Should I hide vegetables in food?
It can be one practical tool, but it should not be the only strategy. Variety grows best when foods also become familiar — seen, touched, tasted and served without pressure over time. Nutrition support can help you combine both approaches without mealtime battles.
Mealtimes have become stressful. Where do we start?
Usually with lowering the pressure: predictable routines, one shared meal where possible, and removing the job of “getting the child to eat” from the parent’s shoulders. Small changes to structure and environment often calm the table before any changes to the menu.
Do you work with teenagers directly?
Support is family-centred and primarily educational. With teenagers, the focus is often on respectful conversations, autonomy at the table and building healthy routines — while keeping parents supported in the background.
Is this medical advice?
No. Wellness Journey provides nutrition education and practical support. It does not replace medical assessment, diagnosis or treatment. If there are growth, swallowing, allergy or other clinical concerns, please work alongside your physician.
